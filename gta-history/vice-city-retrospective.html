GTA History

Vice City Retrospective: Why It Still Holds Up

Leonida Report Staff

Released just a year after GTA III, Vice City took the new 3D engine and used it to build something with far more personality: a neon-drenched, synth-pop-scored parody of 1980s Miami that instantly became one of gaming's most recognizable settings.

Style over scale

Vice City wasn't a huge technical leap over GTA III, but it didn't need to be. Every design decision, from the pastel color palette to the killer licensed soundtrack to Tommy Vercetti's Scarface-inspired rise through the city's underworld, served one consistent aesthetic vision. It remains one of the best examples in gaming of style functioning as substance.

Tommy Vercetti

Ray Liotta's performance as Tommy Vercetti gave GTA its first genuine movie-star protagonist, trading Claude's total silence for a character with real presence and menace. The shift toward stronger character writing here set the template Rockstar would keep building on for the rest of the series.

The soundtrack that defined a decade

Vice City's radio stations are still held up as the gold standard for GTA soundtracks. The 1980s licensed music didn't just play in the background, it actively shaped the mood of every drive through the city, and it remains one of the most nostalgic elements of the entire series for longtime fans.

Why it matters for GTA 6

GTA 6's return to Vice City, this time inside the larger fictional state of Leonida, is leaning directly on the goodwill this game built over twenty years ago. Rockstar knows exactly what fans loved about the original Vice City, and the marketing for GTA 6 has already made clear how much of that spirit is coming back.
