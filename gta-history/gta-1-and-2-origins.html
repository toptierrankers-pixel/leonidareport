GTA 1 and 2: The Top-Down Origins

Leonida Report Staff
GTA HistoryGTA 1GTA 2

It's easy to forget, especially for anyone who joined the series in the 3D era, that Grand Theft Auto started as a top-down game with a fixed camera angle and a mission structure closer to an arcade game than the cinematic crime sagas Rockstar makes today.

Grand Theft Auto (1997)

The original game dropped players into a top-down city and let them steal cars, complete missions for crime bosses, and cause chaos, all wrapped in a darkly comedic tone that would define the series going forward. Its open, non-linear structure was genuinely radical for 1997, even if the presentation looks primitive today.

Grand Theft Auto 2 (1999)

The sequel refined the formula with a gang reputation system, letting players build standing with different criminal factions in a near-future dystopian city. It was a clear iteration rather than a reinvention, and it would end up being the last GTA game built around the top-down perspective before the series jumped into full 3D.

Why these games still matter

The DNA of every later GTA game is visible here: open-ended mission structures, morally murky protagonists, dark satire, and a willingness to let players cause chaos outside the main story. GTA III gets credit for inventing the modern open-world genre, but the design philosophy it built on was already fully formed in these two earlier games.

Curious where it all began? The classic GTA titles are cheap and worth the history lesson.
