GTA History

GTA V a Decade Later: Why It Never Died

Leonida Report Staff

Grand Theft Auto V launched in 2013 and, remarkably, is still one of the best-selling and most actively played entertainment products in the world more than a decade later. Understanding why requires looking at both the base game and the online ecosystem built on top of it.

Three protagonists, one sprawling story

Michael, Franklin, and Trevor gave GTA V a structural trick the series had never attempted before: three fully playable leads whose stories intertwine across a single sprawling crime saga. The character-switching mechanic let Rockstar tell a much larger, more varied story than any single protagonist could carry alone.

Los Santos as a technical showcase

At launch, GTA V's version of Los Santos and Blaine County was one of the most detailed open worlds ever built, and the game's re-releases across three console generations kept pushing that fidelity further. Few games have received this level of sustained technical investment years after their original release.

GTA Online changed everything

What actually kept GTA V commercially dominant for over a decade wasn't the base story, it was GTA Online. Rockstar's continuous stream of heists, updates, and content drops turned the multiplayer mode into an ongoing platform rather than a launch feature, generating billions in ongoing revenue for Take-Two.

The bar GTA 6 has to clear

GTA V's staying power is exactly why expectations for GTA 6 are so extreme. Rockstar isn't just following up a great single-player game, it's following up one of the most commercially successful entertainment products ever created, sustained by over a decade of live updates. That is a genuinely difficult act to follow.
