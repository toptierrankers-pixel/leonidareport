GTA History

San Andreas: The Most Ambitious GTA Ever Made

Leonida Report Staff

Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas, released in 2004, remains the most ambitious entry in the series before the jump to HD consoles. Three full cities, a sprawling rural map connecting them, and RPG-lite systems layered on top of the core GTA formula made it feel massive even by today's standards.

CJ and Grove Street

Carl "CJ" Johnson's return to Los Santos after his mother's murder anchors one of the best-written stories in the series. The Grove Street Families storyline, dealing with gang loyalty, betrayal, and family, gave San Andreas an emotional core that many fans still consider the series' high point.

Three cities, one map

Los Santos, San Fierro, and Las Venturas, each a clear stand-in for a real West Coast city, gave San Andreas a sense of scale nothing else in gaming had attempted at that point. The rural stretches connecting them, filled with their own side content, made the whole map feel lived-in rather than like three theme parks stitched together.

RPG mechanics before it was common

San Andreas let players manage CJ's fitness, appearance, gang territory, and relationships in ways that were unusual for an action game at the time. Some of these systems were divisive, but they showed Rockstar's willingness to experiment well beyond the core mission-and-chaos formula.

Its lasting influence

San Andreas is still widely cited as one of the best games of its console generation, and its ambition set a bar Rockstar has clearly kept chasing ever since. GTA 6's multi-region Leonida map, with its varied environments and larger scope, feels like a spiritual continuation of what San Andreas first attempted two decades ago.
