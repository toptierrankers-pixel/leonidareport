GTA Cheat Codes: A History From GTA III to GTA V

Leonida Report Staff
GuidesGTA IIISan AndreasGTA V

Cheat codes have been a core part of GTA's identity for over two decades, letting players spawn tanks, change the weather, or unleash total chaos at will. Here's how they evolved across the series.

The typed-code era

Starting with GTA III, players entered cheat codes directly during gameplay using specific button or key sequences, unlocking effects like infinite ammo, changed weather, or spawning specific vehicles. Vice City and San Andreas expanded the list significantly, and San Andreas in particular became famous for a huge, often-memorized library of codes fans still recall today.

The GTA IV shift

GTA IV moved cheat codes into Niko's in-game cell phone, letting players dial specific numbers to trigger effects instead of memorizing button sequences. It was a clever way to keep the feature relevant on a new generation of consoles with different control conventions.

GTA V's phone-based system

GTA V kept the phone-dialing approach from GTA IV, letting players trigger effects like invincibility, weapon sets, or vehicle spawns through the in-game phone. It also kept controller button-sequence codes as an option, giving players a choice depending on platform.

Why cheat codes matter to the culture

Codes like San Andreas's famous tank-spawning or GTA V's flying-car cheat became genuine cultural touchstones, referenced constantly outside the games themselves. They're part of why GTA has always felt like a sandbox for chaos as much as a structured crime story.

What to expect from GTA 6

Rockstar hasn't confirmed a cheat system for GTA 6 yet, but given the feature's presence in every mainline entry since GTA III, it would be a genuine surprise if it disappeared entirely.
