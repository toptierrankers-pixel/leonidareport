New to GTA? A Beginner's Guide to the Whole Series

Leonida Report Staff
GuidesGTA VSan AndreasVice City

With GTA 6 driving a wave of new interest in the series, plenty of people are coming to Grand Theft Auto for the first time. Here's how to actually approach it without getting lost in twenty-five years of games.

Start with GTA V

If you only play one game before GTA 6, make it GTA V. It's the most polished, most accessible entry, it's still being actively supported through GTA Online, and it's the direct predecessor to GTA 6 in terms of scale and tone. Nearly everything about how GTA 6 will feel can be understood by playing GTA V first.

Then go back to San Andreas

If GTA V clicks, San Andreas is the next stop. It's the series' most beloved earlier entry, with one of its best stories, and it shows where a lot of the DNA in GTA V's world design actually came from.

Vice City for the aesthetic GTA 6 is chasing

Since GTA 6 is returning to Vice City inside its new Leonida setting, playing the original Vice City gives useful context for what Rockstar is drawing on. It's shorter and more focused than San Andreas or GTA V, which makes it an easy weekend playthrough.

Skip the early top-down games unless you're curious about history

The original GTA and GTA 2 are historically important but rough to actually play today. They're worth reading about rather than playing first, unless you specifically want the full historical context.

What about GTA IV?

GTA IV is worth playing, but it's a tonal outlier, grittier and more serious than the rest of the series. It's better appreciated after you understand what the series usually feels like, rather than as a starting point.

Quickest path in: GTA V, then San Andreas, then Vice City. That covers the series' best storytelling, its most ambitious entry, and the aesthetic GTA 6 is built around.

Starting your GTA journey? Grab GTA V and the rest of the series for less before you dive in.
