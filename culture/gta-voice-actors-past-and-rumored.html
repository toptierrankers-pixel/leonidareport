Who Plays Who: GTA Voice Actors, Past and Rumored for GTA 6

Leonida Report Staff
CultureGTA 6Characters

Voice acting has quietly become one of the most important parts of what makes a GTA protagonist work. A great script only goes so far without a performance to sell it, and Rockstar has had genuine hits and one deliberate non-performance across the series' history. Here's who's voiced who, and what's being said about who might voice Jason and Lucia in GTA 6.

Claude: no voice actor, on purpose

Grand Theft Auto III's Claude never speaks a word, a deliberate design choice rather than a casting gap. Rockstar wanted players to project themselves fully onto the character, and a silent lead was the cleanest way to do that in 2001. It's a choice the series has never gone back to since.

Tommy Vercetti: Ray Liotta

Ray Liotta brought real star power to Vice City, mixing charisma and menace in a way that made Tommy feel like a genuine movie lead rather than a game character. It remains one of the most celebrated voice performances in the series' history, and part of why Vice City's reputation has held up so well over two decades.

Carl "CJ" Johnson: Young Maylay

Young Maylay voiced CJ in San Andreas, balancing toughness with real emotional vulnerability, essential to selling CJ's family drama and his complicated return to Grove Street. The performance is a big part of why CJ is still cited as one of the series' best-written leads.

Niko Bellic: Michael Hollick

Michael Hollick took on GTA IV's more serious, world-weary tone as Niko Bellic, a deliberate departure from the louder, more charismatic performances that came before. The restraint in his delivery matched GTA IV's grittier storytelling perfectly.

Michael De Santa: Ned Luke

Ned Luke voiced Michael in GTA V, leaning fully into the character's mid-life crisis energy, equal parts self-pity, dark comedy, and simmering menace. Michael's scenes are consistently some of the most quoted in the game, largely on the strength of this performance.

Franklin Clinton: Shawn "Solo" Fonteno

Shawn Fonteno voiced Franklin in GTA V, bringing a lived-in authenticity to the role that came from Fonteno's real history working with Rockstar on South Los Santos-set projects. It's one of the more understated performances in GTA V, and one of its most consistently praised.

Trevor Philips: Steven Ogg

Steven Ogg's performance as Trevor is frequently ranked among the best voice performances in gaming, period. He found real, damaged humanity underneath Trevor's chaos rather than playing him as a pure cartoon, which is a big part of why the character works as well as he does.

GTA 6: who might voice Jason and Lucia?

Rockstar has not officially confirmed voice casting for Jason or Lucia as of the trailers released so far. That hasn't stopped the fan and leak community from circulating names, and a few have picked up enough traction to be worth covering here, with the heavy caveat that none of this is confirmed by Rockstar.

Lucia: Manny L. Perez rumored

Manny L. Perez has been named in fan speculation as a possible voice behind Lucia, though this remains unconfirmed rumor rather than official casting news. Treat this the same way you'd treat any pre-release casting leak: plausible, circulating widely, but not something Rockstar has verified.

Jason: Dylan Rourke rumored

Dylan Rourke is the name most frequently attached to Jason in fan casting discussions, again based on unconfirmed speculation rather than any official Rockstar statement. As with Lucia's rumored casting, nothing here should be treated as fact until Rockstar or the actor themselves confirms it.

A general rule with any pre-release GTA casting rumor: Rockstar has historically stayed silent on voice cast details until very close to launch, sometimes not confirming names until after release. Expect these rumors to keep shifting until Rockstar says otherwise.

Why voice casting matters this much for GTA 6

Lucia is the series' first playable woman protagonist, and Jason and Lucia's Bonnie and Clyde dynamic depends entirely on the chemistry between two performances rather than one. Whoever ends up in these roles is taking on some of the most scrutinized casting decisions in recent gaming history.
