GTA 6: Everything We Know So Far

Leonida Report Staff · Updated regularly as new details land

This is the one page to bookmark. Every confirmed detail about Grand Theft Auto 6, its release date, setting, protagonists, trailers, gameplay systems, and the ongoing rumor mill, pulled together in one place and kept current as Rockstar shares more. Each section below links out to our full deep-dive if you want to go further on any one piece.

Release date, platforms, and price

GTA 6 was confirmed in early 2022, and the release window has shifted more than once since, most recently sliding into 2026. It's locked in for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, with no confirmed PC release date alongside the console launch, consistent with how Rockstar staggered GTA V's platform rollout a decade ago. Pricing hasn't been officially confirmed, but Take-Two has hinted at a premium price point above prior GTA entries given the game's scale.

Read the full release date breakdown →

The setting: Leonida and the return of Vice City

GTA 6 takes place in Leonida, Rockstar's fictional take on modern Florida, with Vice City rebuilt from the ground up at its center. Trailer footage has shown swampland, dense urban blocks, suburbs, and beaches, suggesting the most environmentally varied GTA map yet, closer in spirit to San Andreas's multi-region structure than GTA V's single metro area.

Read the full map breakdown →

Meet Jason and Lucia

For the first time in mainline GTA history, players get a female protagonist. Lucia and Jason are framed as a Bonnie and Clyde-style duo, bound to each other as much as to the crimes they commit together, a tighter and more personal structure than GTA V's three unconnected leads.

Every trailer so far

Two official trailers have dropped since the reveal, the first confirming the setting and protagonists, the second widening the scope with more of the map, supporting cast, and Rockstar's signature satire updated for the influencer era. Neither has shown direct gameplay footage in the traditional sense.

The Netflix extended look event

Rockstar broke from its usual playbook and partnered with Netflix for an extended look event, streamed August 27, giving fans the most substantial gameplay-adjacent footage yet. The reaction was immediate and, for a fanbase that argues about everything, unusually unified in its excitement.

Read about the event announcement → · Read the full reaction breakdown →

New gameplay systems we've seen

The extended look surfaced several systems that go well beyond what GTA V ever attempted: a Red Dead Redemption 2-style honor system where choices carry real weight, and a realism-driven wanted mechanic where covering your face and swapping your appearance or vehicle after a job actually matters, since witnesses and police can identify specific clothing, vehicles, and which of the two protagonists was involved. Rockstar also appears to be pushing for near-seamless transitions between cutscenes and gameplay, something that could reset expectations for the genre the way GTA III did in 2001.

Read the full mechanics breakdown →

How GTA 6 compares to GTA V

GTA 6 is the first mainline entry built natively for the current console generation, no last-gen port, no remaster baggage, which is a meaningfully different technical starting point than GTA V ever had. The tone, setting, and protagonist structure are shifting too, evolving Rockstar's satire for a very different decade.
