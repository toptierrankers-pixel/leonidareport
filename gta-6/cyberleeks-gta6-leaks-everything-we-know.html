The CyberLeeks GTA 6 Leaks: Everything We Know

Leonida Report Staff
GTA 6LeaksNews

A new round of GTA 6 leaks has been spreading online over the past few days, tied to an account going by the name CyberLeeks. Here's what's being reported so far, and why we're covering the story without amplifying the leaked material itself.

What's reportedly been leaked

Posts circulating under the CyberLeeks name claim to show material pulled from an unreleased GTA 6 build, spreading across social platforms and leak forums the way GTA leaks typically do, fast, fragmented, and difficult to verify in real time. As with any unconfirmed leak, treat specifics with caution until Rockstar addresses it directly, if they address it at all. We won't be reposting or linking to the leaked material here.

This isn't just leaking anymore, it's a business

What sets this round apart from past GTA leaks is the monetization behind it. CyberLeeks has reportedly been promoting a branded coin tied to the leaks, alongside paid polls letting followers vote on what gets leaked next. That's a meaningful escalation from a lone leaker posting for clout, it turns stolen pre-release material into a subscription-style content pipeline, with paying followers effectively commissioning what gets exposed next.

Leaking GTA material has landed people in serious trouble before

This isn't the first time someone has leaked Rockstar's work and paid a steep price for it. The 2022 GTA 6 breach, tied to the Lapsus$ hacking group, ended with one of those responsible handed an indefinite hospital order in 2024 after being found to have committed the offense while experiencing a mental health crisis, a genuinely serious legal outcome, not a slap on the wrist. Reports and images circulating around CyberLeeks now suggest legal consequences may be closing in on this leak as well.

This is a developing story and details are still unconfirmed. We'll update this post as more verified information comes out.

Where we land on this

We get it, the wait for GTA 6 has been long, and every leak feels like a shortcut to the game we're all impatiently waiting for. But we're not going to turn on Rockstar over it. This is a studio that's given us Vice City, San Andreas, GTA V, Red Dead Redemption 2, and more, entire chapters of a lot of our lives, built by thousands of people working for years on something they clearly care about. Leaks built on exploitation and monetized theft don't deserve our support just because we're impatient.

The best way to back the people actually making this game is simple: wait for it properly, and pre-order when you're ready.
