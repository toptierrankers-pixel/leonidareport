GTA 6's Extended Look Has Dropped: Here's What Everyone's Saying

Leonida Report Staff
GTA 6TrailersNews
Age-restricted on YouTube · Watch on YouTube →

The extended look event Rockstar and Netflix promised has come and gone, and the internet has not stopped talking about it since. This wasn't a two-minute teaser cut to a licensed song, it was a real look at how GTA 6 actually plays, and the reaction has been the closest thing to a unified fanbase we've seen since the first trailer dropped back in 2023. Here's a full breakdown of what people are actually saying, section by section.

The graphics debate: "it just looks like GTA V with better lighting"

This is the take that's been getting the most pushback online, and honestly, it deserves the pushback. A vocal corner of the internet is calling the visuals an incremental step up rather than a generational leap, essentially "GTA V with better lighting." That comparison falls apart the second you look at what platform each game was actually built for.

GTA V originally released on PS3 and Xbox 360, hardware that was already aging by 2013, and every version since has been a port or a remaster of that same original build, carried forward across three console generations. Red Dead Redemption 2 was built for PS4 and Xbox One, a full generation newer, but still not the hardware GTA 6 is targeting. GTA 6 is the first mainline Rockstar open-world game built natively for the current console generation from the ground up, no backward compatibility constraints, no last-gen version to also support. Judging it against GTA V's decade-plus-old foundation and calling the improvement "just lighting" undersells exactly how different the underlying engine work here actually is.

New mechanics: covering your tracks is about to matter a lot more

One of the most talked-about additions is how much further Rockstar is pushing the Red Dead Redemption 2-style realism systems. Covering your face during a crime and changing your appearance or vehicle afterward isn't just flavor this time, it looks mechanically important. Witnesses and police can reportedly work off real descriptions: what you were wearing, what you were driving, and crucially, which of the two protagonists was actually seen doing it.

That last part is a genuinely new wrinkle for GTA. With two playable leads sharing a criminal life, the game seems built to track Jason and Lucia as individually identifiable people, not just "the player character." Get seen in Jason's jacket behind the wheel of his car, and it's Jason who's getting flagged, not some generic wanted-level abstraction. That's a level of specificity the series has never attempted before, and it's exactly the kind of system that rewards actually thinking like a criminal instead of just outrunning a star meter.

An honor system, Red Dead style

Fans are also picking up on what looks like a full honor or reputation system carried over from Red Dead Redemption 2's playbook. If that holds up, it's a meaningful shift for GTA specifically, a series that has historically let players cause chaos with basically no lasting narrative consequence beyond a wanted level. A real reputation system means choices actually follow Jason and Lucia around, changing how the world, and possibly each other, respond to who they've become by the time the credits roll.

"This isn't gameplay, this is a movie", the cutscene-to-gameplay seamlessness

The single most-repeated reaction across social media might be some version of "I can't tell where the cutscene ends and the gameplay starts." The transitions shown in the extended look blur that line about as well as anything ever has in the genre, no hard cut, no loading pause, control just quietly returns to the player mid-scene.

Given where hardware and Rockstar's own production pipeline are right now, this feels less like a one-off flex and more like a preview of where the whole industry is headed. Rockstar has always set the bar for keeping players locked in during story beats instead of watching passively, and if this level of seamlessness becomes achievable at scale, don't be surprised if it becomes the new baseline expectation for big-budget open-world games going forward, the same way GTA III reset expectations for open worlds themselves back in 2001.

Where the fanbase landed

Strip away the individual debates over lighting and engine tech, and the overall temperature is about as hot as it's ever been. Between the new identity-based wanted system, an honor system that actually tracks consequences, and gameplay that looks like it was cut from a film, this extended look did exactly what it needed to do: it made an already-exhausted fanbase even more impatient for release day.

Rockstar hasn't confirmed every system mentioned here in official patch-note detail, plenty of this is still being pieced together from the footage itself. Treat mechanic-level specifics as early impressions until Rockstar states them directly.
