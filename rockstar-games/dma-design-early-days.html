Rockstar Games

From DMA Design to Rockstar North: The Early Days

Leonida Report Staff

Long before the Rockstar name existed, a Dundee, Scotland studio called DMA Design was quietly building the game that would eventually become Grand Theft Auto. Understanding DMA Design's story is essential to understanding where Rockstar's identity actually comes from.

DMA Design's origins

DMA Design was founded in 1987 and built its early reputation on the Lemmings series, a completely different kind of game from what it would later become known for. The studio had a track record of clever, systems-driven design well before crime games entered the picture.

Race'n'Chase becomes Grand Theft Auto

The project that became GTA started under the working title Race'n'Chase. According to accounts from the development team, the original concept leaned more heavily into a cops-and-robbers chase game, but bugs in the traffic AI led to unexpectedly chaotic, funny behavior that the team decided to embrace rather than fix. That happy accident became the seed of GTA's entire open-ended chaos-driven design philosophy.

The DMA to Rockstar transition

Take-Two acquired DMA Design in 1999 following the original Grand Theft Auto's surprise success and the formation of the Rockstar Games label. DMA Design was rebranded as Rockstar North in 2002, cementing it as the lead studio behind the mainline GTA series, a role it has held ever since.

Why this history still matters

Rockstar North, the direct descendant of that original Scottish studio, remains the lead developer on every mainline GTA game today, including GTA 6. The chaotic, emergent, systems-first design philosophy born from a traffic AI bug in the late 1990s is still recognizably the DNA running through every GTA game since.
